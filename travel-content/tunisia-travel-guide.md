# Tunisia: 120 Hidden Gems, Best Itineraries & Traveller Tips

> Web-researched travel guide for the **e-tunisia** project. Compiled June 2026 from current travel sources (see [Sources](#sources)). The companion machine-readable file is [`tunisia-travel-data.json`](tunisia-travel-data.json).

Tunisia packs an absurd amount into a small, easy-to-cross country: Roman cities you can have to yourself, blue-and-white Mediterranean villages, Saharan oases and Star Wars sets, Berber cliff villages, and a coastline that runs from cork-oak forests in the north to palm-fringed islands in the south. This guide focuses on the **off-the-beaten-path** side of all of it.

**How to use this:** the 120 gems are grouped into 8 regions, roughly north → south. Each entry has what it is, why it's worth the detour, and a practical tip. Then come ready-to-run [itineraries](#itineraries) and [traveller tips](#traveller-tips).

## 120 Hidden Gems by Region

### A. Greater Tunis, Carthage & Zaghouan

1. **Souk des Chéchias (Tunis Medina)** — A hushed, centuries-old covered souk where artisans still hand-press the red felt *chéchia* cap. *Tip: go mid-morning when the workshops are open; it's a short walk from the Zitouna Mosque.*
2. **Tourbet El Bey** — The lavish mausoleum of the Husainid beys, all marble columns and Italianate tilework, almost always empty. *Tip: combine with Dar Ben Abdallah next door.*
3. **Dar Ben Abdallah (Museum of Tunisois Arts & Traditions)** — A grand 18th-century palace recreating bourgeois Tunis life around a tiled courtyard.
4. **Zitouna Mosque rooftop terraces** — The carpet-shop rooftops surrounding the Great Mosque give the best free panorama over the medina's sea of white domes. *Tip: shopkeepers let you up hoping you'll browse — a small thank-you or purchase is polite.*
5. **Ennejma Ezzahra — Baron d'Erlanger Palace (Sidi Bou Saïd)** — A dreamlike Arab-Andalusian palace and music museum above the sea, far quieter than the village's main drag.
6. **Dar El Annabi (Sidi Bou Saïd)** — A traditional family mansion open to visitors, with rooftop views over the blue-and-white houses and the Gulf of Tunis.
7. **Café des Nattes & Café Sidi Chabaane (Sidi Bou Saïd)** — Two legendary cliff cafés — one reached by famous blue steps, the other terraced over the marina. *Tip: come for sunset and order a pine-nut mint tea (*thé aux pignons*).*
8. **Antonine Baths (Carthage)** — The largest Roman baths in Africa, right on the shoreline; the surviving columns against the sea are spectacular and rarely crowded.
9. **Tophet of Carthage** — A poignant, little-visited Punic sanctuary of stelae linked to Carthage's most debated rituals.
10. **Byrsa Hill & Carthage National Museum** — The acropolis of ancient Carthage, with Punic foundations and a hilltop museum overlooking the whole bay.
11. **Café Saf-Saf (La Marsa)** — A 13th-century café built around a working well once turned by a camel; a true neighbourhood institution.
12. **La Goulette** — Tunis's seafood port: grilled fish along the Franklin Roosevelt strip and the Ottoman **Karraka** fortress guarding the channel.
13. **Bardo National Museum** — Home to the world's richest collection of Roman mosaics in a former beylical palace. Not "hidden," but criminally skipped by beach tourists.
14. **Temple des Eaux, Zaghouan** — A monumental Roman water temple at the source that fed Carthage's 132 km aqueduct, set against Djebel Zaghouan.
15. **Thuburbo Majus** — A spacious, low-traffic Roman city with a standing capitol and forum, an easy day trip south of Tunis.
16. **Oudhna (Uthina)** — A Roman colony with a half-buried amphitheatre, cisterns and villa mosaics, almost never on a tour bus route.
17. **Zriba El Olia** — A dramatic abandoned Berber cliff-village near Zaghouan, its stone houses clinging to a gorge above thermal springs.
18. **Jebel Ressas** — A pyramid-shaped limestone mountain ~30 km from Tunis, a favourite local day hike with views back to the capital and the sea.

### B. Cap Bon Peninsula

19. **Korbous hot springs** — A cliffside spa town of seven Roman-era springs; at **Aïn Atrous** scalding mineral water pours straight off the rocks into the cold sea. *Tip: bathe where the hot and cold mix at the shoreline.*
20. **Kelibia Fort (Kasbah) & Mansourah beach** — Tunisia's largest medieval fort over a working fishing harbour, with one of the country's most beautiful white-sand beaches just below. *Tip: pair with the local *muscat* wine.*
21. **Ghar El Kebir, El Haouaria** — Roman quarry caves at the very tip of Cap Bon, carved into the cliffs with sea views to the Zembra islands. *Tip: there are bats — bring a light.*
22. **Kerkouane** — The only Punic city to survive without Roman rebuilding, a UNESCO site famous for its pink-plastered bathtubs. Astonishingly quiet.
23. **Korba Lagoon** — A coastal wetland where flamingos and migratory birds gather; a low-key birdwatching spot most tourists drive past.
24. **Zembra & Zembretta Islands** — A protected biosphere reserve of sheer islands off the Cap Bon tip — boat-trip and diving territory.
25. **Nabeul** — Tunisia's pottery and ceramics capital; come for the big **Friday market** and the orange-blossom *(neroli)* distilleries.
26. **Dar Chaabane El Fehri** — A village of stone-carvers whose chiselled doorways and friezes decorate buildings across Tunisia.
27. **Hammamet Medina & Kasbah** — A compact whitewashed old town wrapped in sea walls; climb the kasbah ramparts at golden hour.
28. **Dar Sebastian (Hammamet)** — A 1920s villa-turned-cultural-centre set in lush gardens that once hosted artists and royalty.
29. **Pupput (Hammamet)** — A Roman residential site with mosaics, tucked between the resort hotels.
30. **Sidi Daoud** — The historic tuna-fishing coast famed for the traditional *mattanza*; quiet villages and rugged shoreline.

### C. Bizerte & the North Coast

31. **Bizerte Old Port (Vieux Port)** — A photogenic Ottoman fishing harbour ringed by pastel houses and cafés — the prettiest old port in Tunisia.
32. **Bizerte Kasbah** — A labyrinthine walled medina you can wander almost alone, with rampart views over the channel.
33. **Spanish Fort of Bizerte** — A 16th-century hilltop fort with sweeping views over the lagoon and Mediterranean.
34. **Lake Ichkeul National Park** — A UNESCO-listed lake and wetland, one of the Mediterranean's key stopovers for migrating birds; climb Jebel Ichkeul for the panorama.
35. **Cap Angela (Ras Engela)** — The northernmost point of the entire African continent, marked by a monument on wild cliffs.
36. **Ghar El Melh** — A lagoon town with Ottoman forts and the *ramli* sand-gardens grown below sea level — a globally recognised agricultural heritage system.
37. **Sidi Ali El Mekki** — A wild dune-backed beach near a clifftop shrine, beloved by locals for its turquoise water.
38. **Raf Raf beach & Pilau Island** — A long crescent of white sand facing a sugarloaf island just offshore.
39. **Cap Serrat** — A remote, forest-backed beach where the Kroumirie woods meet the sea — barely any development.
40. **Sejnane** — An unassuming town that's the home of UNESCO-listed **Sejnane pottery**, hand-built and pit-fired by Berber women. *Tip: buy figurines directly from the makers.*
41. **Utica** — Once a great Punic and Roman port (now stranded inland by silt), with a House of the Cascade mosaic and a small museum.

### D. The Northwest: Kroumirie & the Tell

42. **Tabarka — Genoese Fort** — A 16th-century fortress on an islet linked to town, emblem of this coral-coast resort.
43. **Les Aiguilles (Tabarka)** — Jagged "needle" rock formations rising from the sea; the surrounding waters hide rare red coral, making Tabarka Tunisia's diving capital.
44. **Aïn Draham** — A cool, misty mountain town in cork-oak and pine forest — Tunisia's greenest corner and a base for forest hiking. *Tip: best in spring/autumn; locals come to escape the coastal heat.*
45. **Hammam Bourguiba** — A forested thermal spa resort near the Algerian border, long used for its curative waters.
46. **El Feija National Park** — Cork-oak highlands sheltering the rare Barbary deer; trails and a watchtower viewpoint.
47. **Aïn Soltane (Ghardimaou)** — A green spring-fed oasis valley in the far northwest, an offbeat picnic and walking spot.
48. **Bulla Regia** — Extraordinary Roman town where wealthy families built **underground villas** to beat the heat — descend into rooms with mosaic floors still in place. *Tip: the House of Amphitrite mosaic is the highlight.*
49. **Chemtou (Simitthus)** — Ancient Roman **marble quarries** (the prized golden *giallo antico*) with an excellent modern museum.
50. **Dougga (Thugga)** — UNESCO calls it the best-preserved Roman small town in North Africa; a hillside of temples, theatre and the Capitol you can often have nearly to yourself. *Tip: arrive at opening for empty ruins and soft light.*
51. **Thuburnica** — A remote Roman site whose ancient bridge is reportedly still in use by villagers.
52. **Le Kef (El Kef)** — A spectacular cliff-edge town stacked under a kasbah, with a layered history of Numidians, Romans and Ottomans.
53. **Basilica of Dar El Kous (Le Kef)** — A remarkably intact 4th-century Christian basilica in the heart of the old town.
54. **Jugurtha's Tableland (Kalaat Es Senan)** — A vast flat-topped mesa rising over 1,200 m, named for the Numidian king who defied Rome here; a memorable hike to ruins and rock chapels on top.
55. **Hammam Mellègue** — Roman thermal baths in a river valley near Le Kef where hot water still flows into the ancient pools.
56. **Mustis (El Krib)** — Compact roadside Roman ruins — arches, mausolea and an oil press — easy to combine with Dougga.
57. **Makthar (Mactaris)** — A high-plateau site blending Numidian, Punic and Roman remains, including the grand Schola Juvenum.
58. **Testour** — A town founded by Andalusian Muslim refugees, with a unique mosque whose minaret carries a clock that runs anti-clockwise. *Tip: visit during the malouf music festival if timing allows.*
59. **Béja** — A hilltop wheat-country town with a tangled medina, Ottoman kasbah and Byzantine fort.

### E. The Centre & the Sahel Coast

60. **Sousse Medina & Ribat** — A UNESCO-listed walled medina with a 9th-century **ribat** (fortified monastery) you can climb for rooftop views.
61. **Sousse Catacombs** — Kilometres of early-Christian burial galleries beneath the city — atmospheric and almost unvisited.
62. **Monastir Ribat & Bourguiba Mausoleum** — A photogenic fortress (a *Life of Brian* filming location) beside the gilded mausoleum of Tunisia's founding president.
63. **Lamta (Leptiminus)** — A small Roman port site and museum on the coast south of Monastir, well off the tourist map.
64. **Mahdia Medina & Skifa El Kahla** — A peninsular old town entered through a colossal dark vaulted gate; even in summer it stays calm. *Tip: the cape-end Fatimid cemetery meets the sea — beautiful at sunrise.*
65. **Cap d'Afrique & Mahdia fishing port** — Working harbour and lighthouse cape where the day's catch comes in.
66. **El Jem Amphitheatre** — A staggering UNESCO Roman colosseum that seated 35,000, rising straight out of a small modern town — and far emptier than Rome's. *Tip: explore the underground gladiator passages.*
67. **El Jem Archaeological Museum** — In-situ Roman villas and one of Tunisia's best mosaic collections, a short walk from the arena.
68. **Great Mosque of Uqba (Kairouan)** — The oldest and most important mosque in North Africa, in Islam's fourth-holiest city (UNESCO). *Tip: dress modestly; non-Muslims can enter the courtyard.*
69. **Aghlabid Basins (Kairouan)** — Vast 9th-century circular reservoirs — early Islamic hydraulic engineering at scale.
70. **Zaouia of Sidi Sahab — "Mosque of the Barber" (Kairouan)** — A dazzling tiled mausoleum complex honouring a companion of the Prophet.
71. **Raqqada Museum of Islamic Art** — Set in a former presidential palace near Kairouan, holding early Qur'ans and ceramics.
72. **Sfax Medina** — A big, working, utterly untouristed medina — "the real thing," all commerce and local life behind ochre walls.
73. **Kerkennah Islands** — Flat, palm-scattered islands ~90 min by ferry from Sfax, where the pace is slow, the seafood (octopus!) is superb, and the **charfia** palm-frond fishing technique is UNESCO-listed. *Tip: rent a bike; there are no resorts.*
74. **Hergla** — A sleepy clifftop fishing village north of Sousse with whitewashed houses, esparto-weaving and no tourist malls.
75. **Salakta (Sullecthum)** — A quiet coastal village with Roman port remains and a small museum (notably a lion mosaic).
76. **Takrouna** — A tiny Berber village perched on a rocky outcrop near Enfidha, with a shrine on top and huge plains views.

### F. Jerid Oases, the Chott & the Sahara Gateway

77. **Ouled El Hadef quarter (Tozeur Medina)** — A 14th-century quarter famous for ornate **patterned-brick** façades — a building style found nowhere else.
78. **Tozeur Palm Grove** — A vast oasis of 200,000+ date palms with shaded irrigation walks and the "Paradise" garden.
79. **Tozeur Belvédère rocks** — A rocky outcrop above the oasis, the classic spot for sunset over the palms and the salt flats.
80. **La Corbeille de Nefta** — A crater-like hollow of springs and palms within the town of Nefta — the heart of this Sufi oasis.
81. **Nefta old town & Sufi shrines** — Domed white *zaouias* and brickwork lanes; one of Tunisia's spiritual centres and once a Saharan caravan hub.
82. **Chebika** — A mountain oasis where a spring-fed stream and small waterfall spill below a ruined cliff village; views over the Chott. *Tip: walk up the palm gorge early before tour groups arrive.*
83. **Tamerza** — The largest mountain oasis, with a canyon, a year-round **waterfall** and the dramatic ruins of the old village abandoned after 1969 floods.
84. **Mides Canyon** — A deep, twisting gorge on the Algerian border (a *English Patient* location) with a clifftop village above.
85. **Chott El Djerid** — A vast salt lake you cross on a raised causeway, shimmering with mirages and salt crystals in pink, white and ochre. *Tip: dawn or dusk for the colours; don't walk on the soft crust off the road.*
86. **Ong Jemel & Mos Espa** — A "camel's neck" rock formation overlooking the dunes, beside the still-standing **Mos Espa** Star Wars set. *Tip: needs a 4×4 or guide from Tozeur; go early to avoid heat and crowds.*
87. **Sidi Bouhlel ("Star Wars Canyon")** — A narrow desert canyon used for Jawa and Tusken Raider scenes in the original film.
88. **Lars Homestead dome (Chott El Gharsa)** — The restored igloo-shaped exterior of Luke Skywalker's home, sitting alone on the salt flats.
89. **Douz** — The "Gateway to the Sahara," with a Thursday camel market and the December International Sahara Festival; where the palm groves give way to open dunes. *Tip: base here for sunset camel treks into the Grand Erg Oriental.*
90. **Ksar Ghilane** — A remote spring-fed oasis at the edge of the dunes with a natural **hot spring** pool — the classic desert-camp stop. *Tip: a 4×4 is strongly recommended for the final stretch.*
91. **Tisavar Roman fort (Ksar Ghilane)** — A ruined Roman frontier *(limes)* fort a short trek from the oasis — proof Rome reached the desert's edge.
92. **Zaafrane & Sabria** — Saharan villages south of Douz where the true dune sea begins; departure points for camel and 4×4 expeditions.
93. **Kebili** — The main oasis town of the Nefzaoua, with date plantations and nearby hot springs — a workaday alternative base to Douz.

### G. Gabès, Matmata & the Dahar Ksour

94. **Gabès** — The only **seaside oasis** in the Mediterranean, where date palms meet the beach; explore the Chenini-Gabès springs and the henna souk.
95. **Matmata** — Berber **troglodyte** village of homes dug as pit-craters into the earth; the **Sidi Driss hotel** was the Lars family interior in Star Wars. *Tip: you can stay overnight in a cave guesthouse.*
96. **Tamezret** — A Berber hilltop village of stone houses with a café terrace looking over the desert plateau.
97. **Toujane** — A mountain village split across a gorge, known for kilim weaving and switchback views toward the coast.
98. **Chenini** — The most photogenic of the Tataouine Berber villages: cave dwellings beneath a ridgetop *kalaa* and the white **Mosque of the Seven Sleepers**. *Tip: hire a local guide to reach the upper fortress.*
99. **Douiret** — A strikingly beautiful abandoned cliff-village with cave homes, an underground mosque and a hilltop *ksar*.
100. **Guermessa** — The "forgotten" fourth Berber village — twin peaks topped by ruins, with far fewer visitors than Chenini or Douiret.
101. **Ghomrassen** — A valley town of cave dwellings, said to be the birthplace of *fricassé* (the beloved Tunisian fried sandwich).
102. **Ksar Ouled Soltane** — The most spectacular **ghorfa** granary: courtyards stacked four storeys high with vaulted storage cells (a Star Wars slave-quarters location). *Tip: late afternoon light is best for the honeycomb façades.*
103. **Ksar Hadada** — A restored fortified granary used as Mos Espa slave quarters in *The Phantom Menace*.
104. **Ksar Hallouf** — A hilltop ksar with a small museum and panoramic views, well off the standard loop.
105. **Metameur** — A three-courtyard ghorfa complex just outside Médenine, partly turned into a simple guesthouse.
106. **Ksar Médenine (Ksour quarter)** — The remnants of Médenine's once-huge granary city in the town centre.
107. **Beni Kheddache** — A market town high in the Dahar, a gateway to a string of crumbling hill ksour.
108. **Ksar Jouamaa** — A scenic ridge-top ksar near Beni Kheddache with sweeping plateau views.
109. **Tataouine souk** — The regional hub's lively Sunday/Thursday markets — Berber blankets, dates and desert provisions before heading into the ksour.

### H. Djerba & the Far-South Coast

110. **Djerbahood (Erriadh)** — A traditional village transformed into an open-air street-art gallery, with 250+ murals on whitewashed walls. *Tip: wander the back lanes — many of the best pieces aren't on the main route.*
111. **El Ghriba Synagogue (Erriadh)** — One of Africa's oldest synagogues and a major pilgrimage site, testament to Djerba's 2,000-year Jewish community.
112. **Guellala** — Djerba's pottery village, where artisans still pit-fire clay by ancient methods; the hilltop museum has island-wide views.
113. **Houmt Souk old town & Borj El Kebir** — Djerba's whitewashed market town, with vaulted *fondouks* (caravanserais) and a 15th-century seafront fort.
114. **Ajim** — A working sponge-fishing port that doubled as **Mos Eisley** (and Obi-Wan's exterior) in the original Star Wars.
115. **Flamingo Island (Ras R'mel)** — A sandspit off northern Djerba you can walk to at low tide, with flamingos in season.
116. **Sidi Yati beach** — A long, shallow, near-empty beach on Djerba's quieter southeast coast.
117. **Djerba's white mosques (Sidi Jmour & Fadhloun)** — Dozens of squat, fortress-like underground mosques dot the island; Sidi Jmour is a favourite for sunset over the sea.
118. **Zarzis** — A laid-back mainland coast town of long beaches and ancient olive groves, far from the resort bustle.
119. **Gightis (Bou Grara)** — Roman port ruins on the Gulf of Bou Grara near the Djerba causeway, with a forum and temple platform and almost no visitors.
120. **La Galite Islands** — Tunisia's most remote destination: a tiny volcanic archipelago ~60 km off the north coast, with crystal water and a handful of inhabitants — reachable only by special boat trips.

## Itineraries

> Distances are short by African standards, but mountain and desert roads are slow. A **rental car** gives the most freedom; a **4×4** is recommended only for Ksar Ghilane and dune tracks beyond Douz. Allow buffer time — louages (shared taxis) and trains connect the main cities cheaply if you don't drive.

### 1) The Classic Loop — 7 Days (North + Sahara taster)
Best for first-timers who want history, a city, and one desert night.
- **Day 1 — Tunis & Carthage:** Bardo Museum, Carthage (Antonine Baths, Byrsa), sunset in Sidi Bou Saïd.
- **Day 2 — Dougga & Kairouan:** Roman Dougga in the morning, drive south to holy Kairouan (Great Mosque, Aghlabid Basins). Overnight Kairouan.
- **Day 3 — El Jem & Sousse:** The El Jem amphitheatre en route to the Sousse medina/ribat. Overnight Sousse or Sfax.
- **Day 4 — Toward the desert:** Long drive via Gabès to **Tozeur**. Sunset at the Belvédère.
- **Day 5 — Mountain oases + Star Wars:** Chebika, Tamerza, Mides; afternoon Ong Jemel & Mos Espa. Overnight Tozeur.
- **Day 6 — Chott + Matmata:** Cross Chott El Djerid; troglodyte Matmata (Sidi Driss). Overnight Matmata or Douz (camel sunset).
- **Day 7 — Return north:** Drive back to Tunis/Hammamet, or fly Tozeur/Djerba → Tunis to save a long day.

### 2) The Grand Tour — 10 Days (North loop)
Adds the Roman northwest and the deep south. Adapted from a popular 10-day driving route.
- **Day 1:** Tunis → Bizerte → Cap Angela → Testour.
- **Day 2:** Testour → Dougga → Bulla Regia → Le Kef.
- **Day 3:** Le Kef → Sbeitla (Sufetula) → Tozeur.
- **Day 4:** Tozeur → Mides, Chebika, Tamerza → Mos Espa & Lars Homestead.
- **Day 5:** Tozeur → Chott El Djerid → Matmata (Sidi Driss) → Toujane.
- **Day 6:** Toujane → Ksar Hallouf → Ksar Hadada → Ghomrassen → Tataouine.
- **Day 7:** Tataouine → Ksar Ouled Soltane → Douiret → Chenini → Tataouine.
- **Day 8:** Tataouine → El Jem → Mahdia.
- **Day 9:** Mahdia → Kairouan → Sousse.
- **Day 10:** Sousse → Hammamet → Tunis.

### 3) The Complete Tunisia — 14 Days
Run the 10-day Grand Tour, then add:
- **Days 11–12 — Cap Bon:** Korbous hot springs, Kerkouane, Kelibia fort & Mansourah beach, El Haouaria caves, Nabeul pottery.
- **Day 13 — North coast & Kroumirie:** Tabarka (Genoese fort, Les Aiguilles) and forested Aïn Draham.
- **Day 14 — Bizerte & Ichkeul:** Lake Ichkeul birdlife, Bizerte old port, back to Tunis.

### 4) Star Wars / Tatooine Trail — 4–5 Days
Tozeur as base. **Ong Jemel & Mos Espa** → **Sidi Bouhlel canyon** → **Lars Homestead dome (Chott El Gharsa)** → **Matmata / Sidi Driss** (Lars interior) → **Ksar Hadada & Ksar Ouled Soltane** (slave quarters) → **Ajim, Djerba** (Mos Eisley). A 4×4 with a local guide is worth it for the off-road sets.

### 5) Roman Ruins Trail — 5–6 Days
**Carthage** → **Oudhna/Thuburbo Majus** → **Dougga** → **Bulla Regia** (underground villas) → **Chemtou** (marble quarries) → **Makthar** → **Sbeitla (Sufetula)** → **El Jem** amphitheatre. Pair with Tunis's **Bardo** at the start for the mosaics that came from these sites.

### 6) Berber South & Ksour — 4–5 Days
From Djerba or Gabès: **Matmata** → **Tamezret** → **Toujane** → **Tataouine** as hub for **Chenini, Douiret, Guermessa** and the granaries **Ksar Ouled Soltane / Ksar Hadada**, finishing with a **Ksar Ghilane** desert-oasis night.

### 7) Slow North-Coast & Nature — 4–5 Days
**Bizerte** old port → **Ichkeul** & **Cap Angela** → **Ghar El Melh** lagoon → **Tabarka** coral coast → **Aïn Draham** & **El Feija** forests. Best in spring or autumn.

## Traveller Tips

### Best time to go
- **Sweet spots: April–June and September–October** — mild 20–25 °C days, good for both ruins and beaches.
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** coast is hot (35 °C+) and busy; the Sahara can exceed 40 °C and see dust storms — desert travel is much better outside summer.
- **Winter:** mild, green north; cool desert days and genuinely cold nights — pack layers for camps.
- **Ramadan (starts mid-Feb in 2026):** many daytime cafés/restaurants close and the pace slows; hotels still serve food. Be discreet about eating/drinking in public during fasting hours.

### Money
- Currency is the **Tunisian dinar (TND)** — a **closed currency**: you can't buy it abroad and can't legally take it out. Exchange on arrival and spend/convert leftovers before you fly home (keep exchange receipts).
- **Cash is king.** Many small shops, cafés, louages, rural guesthouses and desert camps are cash-only. ATMs are common in cities and tourist areas but scarce in the deep south — withdraw before heading into the Sahara.
- Cards work in mid-range/upmarket hotels, supermarkets and some restaurants in tourist zones; don't rely on them elsewhere.

### Getting around
- **Rental car = maximum freedom**, ideal for the itineraries above. Roads between main towns are fine for a normal car; a **4×4** is only really needed for Ksar Ghilane and dunes past Douz.
- **No Uber.** In cities use **Bolt** or **Heetch** apps, or metered yellow taxis (insist on the meter / agree a price first).
- **Louages** (shared inter-city taxis) are cheap, frequent and the local way to travel; **trains** link Tunis–Sousse–Sfax and the TGM connects Tunis–Carthage–La Marsa.
- Drive defensively: expect assertive driving, livestock on rural roads, and avoid unfamiliar mountain/desert roads after dark.

### Safety
- Tunisia is generally safe for visitors; the main risk is **opportunistic petty theft** (pickpocketing, bag-snatching) in crowded souks and transport hubs. Wear bags cross-body, carry little cash, leave passport/valuables in the hotel safe.
- Check your government's current advisory before travel. Standing advice urges **extra caution near the Libya and Algeria borders, the western mountains, and military zones** — avoid these areas unless on a reputable guided trip.
- For desert trips, use an established operator, tell someone your route, and carry plenty of water.

### Culture & etiquette
- **Dress modestly** away from beach resorts — cover shoulders and knees for medinas, mosques and rural villages; light layers work year-round. Mosques may admit non-Muslims to courtyards but not prayer halls; dress respectfully and remove shoes where asked.
- Tunisians are warm and hospitable. **Handshakes** are the norm; let local women initiate any contact, and don't expect hugs from acquaintances.
- **Bargaining** is expected in souks (not in fixed-price shops or restaurants) — stay friendly and smiling. A polite "*La, shukran*" (no, thank you) deflects persistent sellers.
- Ask before photographing people, especially women and inside religious sites.

### Tipping
- Low-key but customary: round up or leave **~10%** in restaurants (2–5 TND in cafés), a few dinars for hotel porters/housekeeping, and tip drivers/guides for good service.

### Language
- Official languages are **Arabic and French**; Tunisian Arabic *(Derja)* is the street language. **French is widely understood**, and English is common in tourist areas. A few Derja words (*aslema* = hello, *shukran* = thanks, *barsha* = a lot) go a long way.

### Connectivity
- Buy a **local SIM/eSIM** on arrival — **Ooredoo** and **Orange** have the best coverage, **Tunisie Telecom** too. SIMs run roughly **5–20 TND** plus a data bundle; bring your passport to register. Coverage is solid in towns and along main roads, patchy deep in the desert.

### Health & practical
- **Tap water:** stick to bottled/filtered, especially in the south.
- **Sun & desert:** high-SPF sunscreen, hat, sunglasses and far more water than you think; sturdy shoes for ruins and canyons. Desert nights get cold — bring a fleece even in shoulder season.
- Carry **toilet paper/hand gel** for rural stops, and small change for parking attendants and *hammam* attendants.
- Standard travel vaccinations; bring any prescription meds with you.

### Food & drink to seek out
- **Brik** (egg-and-tuna fried pastry), **couscous**, **lablabi** (chickpea soup), **ojja** (spicy egg-and-merguez), grilled fish on the coast, **octopus** in Kerkennah, **dates** (Deglet Nour) from the Jerid, **makroudh** pastries from Kairouan, mint tea with pine nuts, and Tunisian wines/*muscat* from Cap Bon. **Harissa** comes with almost everything — it's hot.

### Scams & hassles to sidestep
- Agree taxi fares or insist on the meter before setting off; be wary of "the site is closed, come to my shop instead" detours near medinas and ruins.
- "Free" gifts, henna or guide offers usually aren't — clarify cost up front.
- Only change money at banks, official bureaux or hotels, never on the street.
